Why Truck Accidents Require a Specialist

A collision with an 80,000-pound commercial semi-truck is fundamentally different from a standard car crash. The injuries are often catastrophic, and the legal landscape is heavily regulated by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA).

Trucking companies and their insurers deploy rapid-response defense teams to the scene of an accident immediately. Their goal is to preserve evidence that helps them and mitigate their financial exposure. You need an attorney who can move just as fast to secure driver logs, electronic logging device (ELD) data, black box downloads, and maintenance records before they are destroyed or "lost."

Common Causes of Commercial Truck Crashes

  • Hours of Service (HOS) violations and driver fatigue
  • Improperly loaded or unsecured cargo
  • Negligent hiring and inadequate driver training
  • Falsified logbooks
  • Deferred truck maintenance and brake failures

Act Quickly to Preserve Evidence

Federal regulations only require trucking companies to keep certain records (like driver logs) for six months. If a spoliation letter is not sent immediately, crucial evidence proving negligence can be legally destroyed.
